9. AI Infrastructure and Deployment Exploits
Organizations are deploying Artificial Intelligence (AI) models at a
rapid pace. What was once a research notebook on a data scientist's
laptop now runs as a production service behind load balancers, inside
Kubernetes clusters, and across multi-cloud environments. This
adoption has outpaced security maturity.
In this Learning Module, we focus on
and services that support the deployment and operation of AI models in
This infrastructure introduces an attack surface that blends
traditional cloud, container, and networking vulnerabilities with
risks specific to the AI ecosystem. From a red team perspective,
organizations often fail to defend these systems properly, making them
a high-value target. We'll examine the common patterns used to deploy
AI solutions in production and analyze the attack vectors each one

9.1. Cloud Service Misconfigurations
In this Learning Unit, we'll explore how misconfiguration in
cloud-managed AI services can introduce vulnerabilities that attackers
can chain together to abuse other components of the AI/ML stack.

This "managed service" nature also introduces a challenge for a red
team. A mandatory step in any assessment involving cloud services is
setting up boundaries between the cloud provider and the customer
responsibility. Depending on the service the scope of responsibility
can vary, but in general, the cloud provider is responsible for the
security of the underlying infrastructure, while the customer is
responsible for securing their data, access controls, and

looking for misconfigurations and vulnerabilities in the customer's
domain. Overly permissive roles, exposed endpoints, and leaked
credentials are common across cloud AI/ML deployments, and we'll learn
how to find and exploit each of them in the following Learning Units.
